# Offline knowledge-source options

Research snapshot: 2026-10-28. This is a source survey for Planner; it makes no content-selection decision. Primary source URLs below were checked remotely on the snapshot date. No source was downloaded locally and no local performance/storage measurement exists. URLs may evolve; preserve source URL, exact edition/revision, license and retrieval date with any acquired pack.

### Medical-source labeling requirement

Original publisher documents should remain directly openable/searchable as source records. Generated answers/summaries should be a separate view and never inherit an “official”, “FDA-approved”, or clinician-reviewed label. DailyMed is NLM-hosted company-submitted in-use labeling and explicitly warns that it may not equal current FDA-approved labeling; FDA Drugs@FDA should be consulted for approval status. Attach publisher, jurisdiction, product/document identifier, revision/publication date, retrieval timestamp and source URL to every result. If a source has no review state, label it as unreviewed rather than implying clinical validation.

## Verification boundaries and open questions for Planner

- No local measurements, downloads, installations, licenses/legal opinions, or clinical review were performed.
- Exact current Kiwix archive sizes for selected ZIMs and actual language availability need a live catalog lookup when Planner chooses candidates.
- Exact license needs per file/edition for Scala, WHO, government manuals, media, and drug labels; corpus-level assumptions are insufficient.
- Which jurisdictions/languages are required for first aid, medical references, recipes, and survival guidance? Those materially affect authoritative source choice.
- Should proprietary/commercial distribution be assumed? This changes whether NC (iFixit, many WHO works) material is viable.
- Should medical content include only first-aid/public-health references, or also drug labeling? Label corpora are large, fast-changing, and require explicit jurisdiction/version handling.
